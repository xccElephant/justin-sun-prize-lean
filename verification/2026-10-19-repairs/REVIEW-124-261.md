## 124: gcd obstruction

Reviewed `JspBounties/Erdos124.lean:687–724`, the unchanged `sumsOfDistinctPowers` definition, and FC `erdos124.ne_zero`.

For k≠0, every sum from one base is divisible by d^k and therefore by d. Consequently every represented integer from the finite-base sumset is divisible by D.gcd id. If every sufficiently large natural is represented, both N and N+1 are represented; their common gcd divisor divides 1. This proves D.gcd id=1 with no reciprocal-sum assumption or lower bound on bases. The empty-D case is not silently excluded: eventual coverage of its zero-only sumset is impossible, so the implication remains sound. The k≠0 premise is essential and matches the positive-cutoff branch; the result is not extended to k=0.

`necessary_conditions` combines this obstruction with the established reciprocal-sum converse under bases≥3. It concludes only `1 ≤ reciprocal sum ∧ gcd=1`; it does not invert that implication. No core sufficiency statement is assumed to prove itself.

## 124: residue representatives

Reviewed `residueExponents`, `residue_representative`, and `exists_mem_sumsOfDistinctPowers_modEq` at lines777–816.

External premises are a positive modulus m and Nat.Coprime d m. For every cutoff k and residue r, the exponents are `(k+j+1)*totient(m)` for j<r. Positivity of totient(m) proves injectivity and all exponents≥k. Euler's theorem makes each power congruent to 1 modulo m, so the sum of the r distinct exponent terms is congruent to r. The wrapper supplies actual membership in the original finite distinct-exponent sumset, not merely an unconstrained congruent integer. At r=0 the empty sum is allowed by the definition. At m=1 the sole residue class is handled without an invalid division or totient-zero case.

The representative a depends on d,m,k,r and has no useful target-size bound. A residue representative does not prove that every large integer in that residue class is represented. Nor does gcd(D)=1 ensure there is a single base d∈D coprime to every desired modulus. These gaps prevent promoting the lemma to the complete positive-cutoff or Melfi theorem without further work. The existing bounded-gap lemma likewise gives approximation within C, not exact coverage when C>0.

The Melfi attribution is for the mathematical Euler-period step. The code invokes installed Mathlib's totient theorem; no unstated analytic or completeness hypothesis is present in these additions.

## 261: finite choice cardinality and quantifier order

Reviewed `JspBounties/Erdos261.lean:320–529` and the original FC representation definition/part iii. `Erdos261InfiniteRepresentation` remains StrictMono, positive entries, Summable rational series, and equality of the rational x to the tsum. Reordering a sequence is therefore not counted as a new representation.

For each fixed natural r the proof sets

`x_r = 2 - ∑ i : Fin r, dyadicWeight(blockStart i)`.

This is a rational finite sum. The same x_r is used for **all** bit assignments Fin r→Bool. Each disjoint Borwein–Loring block has equal mass whether it deletes its singleton start or its following interval; therefore every bit assignment deletes exactly the same total mass. Finite deletion from the positive naturals leaves an infinite denominator set, and its order-isomorphic enumeration yields the required strictly increasing positive sequence with a proven rational HasSum. The proof does not rely on assigning an arbitrary tsum to a nonsummable series.

Distinct bit assignments really give distinct representations: each block's start marker records its bit; other blocks cannot contain that marker because the blocks are separated. Equality of the selected sequences would imply equality of ranges and hence equality of every bit. An injective map from Fin r→Bool into the representation subtype gives cardinality 2^r using `mk_arrow`, `mk_bool`, and `mk_fin`. There is no duplicate-counting issue from proof fields or sequence permutations.

The theorem parameter r is **outside** `∃ x`; x_r changes with r. Thus the exact logical statement is `∀ r, ∃ x, 2^r ≤ cardinality(representations x)`. Moving ∃x before ∀r is not licensed. Even a separate theorem asserting all finite bounds for one fixed x would only prove infinite cardinality, not automatically the continuum lower bound required by FC part iii. The new `representations_le_continuum` gives an upper bound for each x and cannot supply that missing lower bound. The interior-remainder auxiliary lemmas explicitly stop short of recurrent branching and do not close the gap.

One minor wording ambiguity: the theorem docstring says “at least that many” after “number of independent binary choices”; the formal type and file introduction correctly specify **2^r** representations. This does not warrant a blocking code change, but reports should write the exact bound.
