## 238: all quantifiers and absence of extra hypotheses

Reviewed `JspBounties/Erdos238.lean:187–248`, its actual diff against HEAD, the local and live FC 238 declarations, the FC primeGap definition, and installed Mathlib `Chebyshev.pi_ge'`.

The new public theorem has exactly the external parameters `(c₁ c₂ : ℝ)`, `0 < c₁`, and `c₂ < 2`. Thus it applies to every positive c₁ and, in particular, every 0 < c₂ < 2. The lack of a c₂-positivity hypothesis strengthens this branch. It has no prime-count bound, selected-x condition, sieve assumption, asymptotic conjecture, or input witness as an external premise.

The conclusion is `∀ᶠ x : ℝ in atTop`; this means there exists a real threshold X after which **every real x ≥ X** works. It is not merely infinitely many x, natural x, or a chosen subsequence. For each such x it gives a natural k with strict `c₁ * log x < k`, a function on Fin k, and one common starting index m with `f i = nth Prime (m+i)`, all f i ≤ x. The adjacent gap indices range over Fin (k−1), exactly the gaps inside that run. The local primeGap is definitionally the same nth-prime difference as FC.

Proof audit: Chebyshev's unconditional lower bound `((x−1)log 2 − log(x+2))/log x ≤ primeCounting(floor x)` is called as a theorem, not assumed. Limits of log x/x and (log x)²/x supply the eventual prime-count surplus. The selected k = floor(c₁ log x)+1 has k > c₁ log x and k ≤ c₁ log x+1. The surplus gives 1+k < primeCounting(floor x), then nth(1+k) ≤ floor x ≤ x. This discharges the former finite-run lemma's explicit endpoint condition inside the proof. Starting after prime 2 makes every internal adjacent gap ≥2, hence >c₂. The internal x≥2 restriction is an eventual bound and adds no premise to the final theorem.

Remaining scope: the unrestricted FC question quantifies over all positive c₂, including c₂≥2. Those cases are not supplied. The small-c₁ solved variant for arbitrary c₂ remains conditional in this repository via `erdos_238_small_c1_of_brun_chebyshev`; this review does not upgrade it.

## 372: literal largest-prime finite existence

Reviewed `JspBounties/Erdos372.lean:233–252`, the actual diff, catalog `reference/awards/problems/catalog-0301-0400.md` JSP-000307, and local/live FC 372.

The new theorem has **no external hypotheses**. Its witnesses are n=13, p=13, q=7, r=5. It requires n>0; p,q,r prime; p|n, q|(n+1), r|(n+2); and each witness dominates **every prime divisor** of its corresponding integer. It then requires r<q<p. These clauses express that p,q,r really are the largest prime factors, rather than arbitrary factors with a descending order. Prime divisibility ensures each corresponding integer exceeds 1, so the conventions at 0 and 1 cannot produce a degenerate witness.

The custom maxPrimeFac function only occurs in the proof of the upper bounds for 14 and 15, not in the theorem statement. Its API proves both primality/divisibility and the domination property on positive nontrivial integers. The sidecar also derives the usual `∃ n>0, maxPrimeFac n > maxPrimeFac(n+1) > maxPrimeFac(n+2)` from the new statement, using its quantified domination clauses rather than trusting the concrete witness alone.

The literal catalog question asks whether three consecutive integers **can** have strictly decreasing largest prime factors, so this finite-existence theorem answers that wording. The FC original is an **infinite set** of decreasing triples. A single witness, separate infinite adjacent comparisons, or infinite valleys `P(n)>P(n+1)<P(n+2)` does not prove that original. The new docstring correctly states that it does not assert infinitude.
