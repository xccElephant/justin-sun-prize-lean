## Scope and original mathematics

The official catalog at `fd8fa66abb4e21df828fdebc25298e9ad75521bd` asks whether finitely many residue classes can cover the integers with no modulus dividing another. The original page's literal question matches Erdős **586** (Schinzel's conjecture), independently of the mapping script. It asks one question; adjacent Erdős–Selfridge odd-covering and minimum-modulus problems are not additional obligations of this entry. Moduli are nontrivial positive integers, and occurrences must be retained: equal moduli on distinct occurrences already give a dividing pair.

The answer is **no**. Balister, Bollobás, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba, *On the Erdős covering problem: the density of the uncovered set*, Invent. Math. (2022), 377–414, DOI **10.1007/s00222-021-01087-5**, prove Schinzel's conjecture. The full author preprint was obtained from https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03547 and saved as `evidence/paper-attempt-1.pdf` with extracted text. Its header explicitly identifies **v1, November 8, 2018**; it must not be described as a freshly fetched final 2022 typeset version. The publisher download did not yield a PDF. The full preprint contains Theorem 1.2 and its proof, not merely an abstract claim; the official bibliography and Crossref record separately establish the publication identity.

## Complete known proof route

Assume a cover whose moduli form a divisibility antichain and pass to a minimal subcover. A prime-power modulus is impossible: deleting its occurrence leaves an uncovered point; shifting by the lcm of the other moduli preserves failure of all other classes and destroys membership in that prime-power class. Thus every modulus has at least two prime factors.

Use the distorted prime-stage sieve (paper Sections 2–3 and 6), beginning with the primes 2,3,5 and zero initial distortions. The small smooth-antichain estimates of Section 9 give removed mass at most `1/3` and the five-smooth second-moment constant `κ=1.7=17/10`. Therefore remaining mass `μ₃ ≥ 2/3` and normalized quantity `f₃ ≤ (17/10)/(2/3)=51/20`. For each later prime, split moduli into a five-smooth factor and the remaining factor; the antichain lcm sum bound and Euler product bound give the second-moment estimate. Lemma 6.2 derives the guarded recurrence and positive next remaining mass. Theorem 1.2's proof uses this seed and Corollary 6.3 to show the sieve survives and the classes cannot cover. The extracted full proof is at `paper-attempt-1.txt:1404–1446`; Lemma 6.2 is at lines 1029–1049. Those proofs and their supporting sections are present in the full PDF. Not every line was independently rederived in this bounded pass.

The fixed Lean implementation uses a different explicitly implemented numerical continuation: distortion `1/5`, a prime certificate through stage 10000 with `f₁₀₀₀₀ < 13000`, followed by a proved analytic tail-budget bound. These constants must be attributed to the inspected implementation, not inaccurately quoted as the preprint's own final calculation.
