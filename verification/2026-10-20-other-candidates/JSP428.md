## Scope and original mathematics

The official catalog at `fd8fa66abb4e21df828fdebc25298e9ad75521bd` asks for the largest pairwise noncoprime subset of `[1,N]` containing `N`. Its bibliography and actual original content agree with Erdős **534**; the mapping file was used only as a lead. The original page and full Ahlswede–Khachatrian paper were obtained (see `evidence/original-534.html` and `evidence/original-paper.pdf`). The author-hosted PDF is https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/ahlswede/homepage/public/109.pdf . Publication: Acta Arith. 75 (1996), 259–276, DOI 10.4064/aa-75-3-259-276.

For `N ≥ 2`, let `q₁ < … < qᵣ` be its distinct prime factors. The answer is

`max_{1 ≤ j ≤ r} |{m ∈ [1,N] : (q₁⋯qⱼ) ∣ m or ∃ i ≤ j, 2qᵢ ∣ m}|`.

The paper's Theorem 1 (extracted text lines 83–93) proves the stronger auxiliary result for arbitrary finite prime sets `Q` and `n ≥ ∏ Q`; specializing to `Q = N.primeFactors` is valid because the squarefree product divides `N`. The original interval endpoint and containing-`N` requirement are both essential. The degenerate `N=1` has only `{1}`, cardinality 1; it is elementary and outside the paper's explicit `n>1` convention, not a new core gap. Neither the infinite-density Theorem 2 nor the unrelated problem 56 is an additional question demanded by this JSP entry.

The older assertion that the maximum equals one of only two quantities is false. A bounded independent integer computation gives `N=2431=11·13·17`: multiples of `22,26,34,2431` inside `[1,2431]` form an admissible family of cardinality 256, while `N/11=221` and the even members sharing a factor with `N` number 255. Distinct even elements share 2; each selected even shares an endpoint prime with `2431`. Thus the refined theorem also resolves, rather than assumes, the old assertion's failure. This computation was not formalized or submitted.

## Complete known proof route

The full paper develops primitive generators, divisibility closure, squarefree reduction and prime compression (Sections 2 and 4), a sifted-set doubling inequality (Theorem 3, Section 3), and its use in Section 5. Choose an optimal compressed family minimizing its largest prime outside `Q`. Partition its top generators into two pull families; the disjoint fiber decomposition and doubling inequality show one pull preserves maximum cardinality while lowering that prime. This forces support into `{2} ∪ Q`. Repeat with the largest endpoint prime to force the generators `2q₁,…,2qⱼ,q₁⋯qⱼ`. The single-prefix case is all multiples of `q₁`. The even endpoint case has the universal `⌊N/2⌋` bound, attained by all evens. The source is a complete published proof, though not every line was independently rederived in this bounded pass.
