## Complete mathematical proof route

Use the quantitative ternary Vinogradov lower bound: for sufficiently large odd n, the sum of log(a)log(b)log(c) over ordered prime triples a+b+c=n is at least c₀n² for some c₀>0. Each weight is at most log(n)³. For a=b, the value a determines c=n−2a, so at most n+1 triples lie on that diagonal; the same holds for a=c and b=c. Their union has at most 3(n+1) triples and weight at most 3(n+1)log(n)³=o(n²). Thus every sufficiently large odd n has a representation by three pairwise distinct primes. This is the analytic input, not an assumption that ordinary three-prime existence alone implies distinctness.

Choose N at least 2 past that threshold, and Q₀ all primes ≤N. Strongly induct on the prime p. If p≤N it belongs to generation zero. Otherwise p is odd and decomposes as p=a+b+c with three distinct primes. Positivity makes each summand strictly smaller than p. Induction reaches each at some finite generation; monotonicity puts all three in the maximum of those generations, and the next generation contains p. Therefore every prime is eventually reached by this one fixed finite seed. Given k, select k distinct primes and synchronize their finite arrival stages at their maximum. That generation has cardinality ≥k. Hence generations are unbounded and their union is infinite. All generations contain only primes by construction.
