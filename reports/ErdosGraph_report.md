## 3. Mathematical & Formalization Techniques

### 3.1. Erdős Problem 23 (`n = 1` and tightness on $5$ vertices)
- **`erdos_23.variants.n1`**: Instead of brute-force enumeration over $2^{10} = 1024$ graphs on `Fin 5`, we gave a short structural proof:
  - **Case 1 ($\exists v, \deg(v) \ge 2$)**: Take the cut $S = N_G(v)$ and let $H = G \sqcap \text{bipFromSet}(S)$. Since $G$ is triangle-free, $S = N_G(v)$ is an independent set in $G$, so no edge of $G$ lies inside $S$. Moreover, $v \notin S$ has all its neighbors in $S$, so any edge of $G$ omitted by $H$ must have both endpoints in $(S \cup \{v\})^c$. Since $|S| \ge 2$ and $v \notin S$, $|(S \cup \{v\})^c| \le 5 - 3 = 2$, which spans at most $\binom{2}{2} = 1$ edge.
  - **Case 2 ($\forall v, \deg(v) \le 1$)**: By the Handshaking Lemma (`sum_degrees_eq_twice_card_edges`), $2|E(G)| = \sum_v \deg(v) \le 5$, so $|E(G)| \le 2$. If $|E(G)| \le 1$, $H = \bot$ omits at most $1$ edge; if $|E(G)| = 2$, picking one edge $e = uv \in E(G)$ and setting $S = \{u\}$ keeps $e$ in $H$ and thus omits at most $2 - 1 = 1$ edge.
- **`erdos_23.variants.n1_tight`**: Witnessed by $G = \text{cycleGraph } 5$ ($C_5$), using `chromaticNumber_cycleGraph_of_odd` to show $\chi(C_5) = 3 > 2$.

### 3.2. Erdős Problem 617 (`r2` infinite family and `r_eq_3` on $K_{10}$)
- **`erdos_617.variants.r2`**: For every prime $p \ge 2$, we take $V = \text{ZMod } p \times \text{ZMod } p$ ($|V| = p^2$) and define the edge coloring $\text{coloring} : \text{Sym2 } V \to \text{Fin } p$ by:
  - Color $0$ if $u_1 = v_1$ (vertical lines) or $u_2 = v_2$ (horizontal lines, slope $0$).
  - Color $m.\text{val} \in \{0, \dots, p-1\}$ where $m = (v_2 - u_2)(v_1 - u_1)^{-1} \in \text{ZMod } p$ if $u_1 \ne v_1$.
  For any subset $S \subseteq V$ with $|S| = p + 1$ and any color $k \in \text{Fin } p$ (corresponding to $m = (k.\text{val} : \text{ZMod } p)$), the linear map $x \mapsto x_2 - m x_1 : S \to \text{ZMod } p$ maps $p + 1$ elements into $p$ values. By the Pigeonhole Principle (`Finset.exists_ne_map_eq_of_card_lt_of_maps_to`), there exist $u \ne v \in S$ with $u_2 - m u_1 = v_2 - m v_1$. Since $u \ne v$, we must have $u_1 \ne v_1$, so $(v_2 - u_2)(v_1 - u_1)^{-1} = m$, giving $\text{coloring } s(u, v) = k$.
- **`erdos_617.variants.r_eq_3`**: Suppose for contradiction that $K_{10}$ has a balanced $3$-edge-coloring (every $4$-subset contains all $3$ colors $0, 1, 2$).
  1. **No $5$-edge $4$-subsets (`no_five_edges_in_four`)**: In any $4$-subset $S$, at most $4$ edges can have color $c$, since the other $2$ colors must also appear among the $6$ edges of $S$.
  2. **Induced properties on non-neighborhoods $W_c(v)$**: For any vertex $v$ and color $c$, let $W_c(v) = \{u \ne v \mid \text{coloring } s(v, u) \ne c\}$. Every $3$-subset of $W_c(v)$ forms a $4$-subset with $v$ and therefore contains at least one $c$-edge (`w_edge_of_three`). Consequently, for any $u \in W_c(v)$, its $c$-non-neighbors in $W_c(v)$ form a $c$-clique, so $u$ has at most $3$ $c$-non-neighbors in $W_c(v)$ (`w_nonneighbors_le_three`). Moreover, if $|W_c(v)| \ge 6$, $u$ has at most $3$ $c$-neighbors in $W_c(v)$ (`w_neighbors_le_three_of_card_ge_six`).
  3. **Exact $3$-regularity in all colors (`w_card_eq_six_and_deg_three`)**: If $|W_k(v)| \ge 7$, taking a $7$-element subset $U_7 \subseteq W_k(v)$ forces every vertex in $U_7$ to have degree exactly $3$ in color $k$ inside $U_7$, which is impossible by the Handshaking Lemma ($7 \times 3 = 21$ is odd). Thus $|W_k(v)| \le 6$ and $|N_k(v)| = 9 - |W_k(v)| \ge 3$ for all $k \in \text{Fin } 3$. Since $|N_0(v)| + |N_1(v)| + |N_2(v)| = 9$, every vertex $v$ has degree **exactly $3$** in every color, and $|W_c(v)| = 6$.
  4. **Final triangle structure contradiction**: By $R(3, 3) = 6$ on $W_0(v)$, there exists a color-$0$ triangle $\{x, y, z\}$ (`exists_triangle_of_bal`). Let $a, b, c$ be the unique third color-$0$ neighbors of $x, y, z$ outside $\{x, y, z\}$, and let $R = V \setminus \{x, y, z, a, b, c\}$ ($|R| = 4$). Using degree-$3$ regularity in $W_0(x), W_0(y), W_0(z)$, we show $\{a, b, c\}$ is a color-$0$ independent set and the color-$0$ neighbor sets $A, B, C \subseteq R$ of $a, b, c$ satisfy $|A|, |B|, |C| \le 2$ together with $R \setminus B \subseteq A$, $R \setminus B \subseteq C$, and $R \setminus A \subseteq C$. This forces $A = R \setminus B$ and $R = (R \setminus B) \cup (R \setminus A) \subseteq C$, contradicting $|R| = 4 > 2 \ge |C|$.

### 3.3. Erdős Problem 628 (`k_5_a_3_b_3` and `independence_number_2`)
- **`erdos_628.variants.k_5_a_3_b_3` (Brown–Jung 1969)**:
  - We represent odd closed walks via periodic sequences `IsCyclicWalk G (2 * m + 1) f`, avoiding cumbersome `SimpleGraph.Walk` path-splicing.
  - Taking a minimum-length odd cyclic walk of length $2m + 1$ (`exists_min_odd_cyclicWalk`):
    - **Case $m \ge 2$ (`colorable_four_of_min_odd_cyclicWalk`)**: Minimality implies $C = \text{range } f$ has no chords (`min_odd_cyclicWalk_adj_iff`) and no external vertex $v \notin C$ is adjacent to $\ge 3$ vertices of $C$ (`min_odd_cyclicWalk_external_le_two`, since the $3$ arcs on $C$ sum to $2m + 1 \ge 5$, so one arc has odd length $\le 2m - 3$). If $V \setminus C$ were $2$-colorable with classes $B_0, B_1$, then $3$-coloring $C$, giving $B_1$ color $3$, and giving each $v \in B_0$ a color in $\{0, 1, 2\}$ unused by its $\le 2$ neighbors in $C$ yields a proper $4$-coloring of $G$, contradicting $\chi(G) = 5$.
    - **Case $m = 1$ (Triangle case)**: By `colorable_four_of_triangle_and_bipartite_compl`, if the complement of any triangle $\{a, b, c\}$ is $2$-colorable, there must exist at least two vertices outside $\{a, b, c\}$ complete to $\{a, b, c\}$ (`exists_fourth_complete_of_not_colorable_four`). Starting from a triangle $\{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ and a fourth vertex $x_4$ forming a $K_4$, three applications of `exists_fourth_complete_of_not_colorable_four` (following Stiebitz's Theorem 2.2(iii)) produce vertices $z_1, z_2, z_3 \notin \{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ forming a triangle $\{z_1, z_2, z_3\}$ disjoint from $\{x_1, x_2, x_3\}$ (since any cross-adjacency that would identify $z_2 = x_1$ or $z_3 = x_2$ would create a $K_5$, contradicting `G.CliqueFree 5`).
- **`erdos_628.variants.independence_number_2` (Balogh–Kostochka–Prince–Stiebitz 2009)**:
  - We prove the general inductive theorem `indep2_splittable_on_set` by strong induction on $n = |U|$:
    1. **Non-critical reduction**: If there exists a non-empty $P \subseteq U$ such that $G[U \setminus P]$ is still not $(k-1)$-colorable, apply the induction hypothesis to $U \setminus P$.
    2. **Disconnected complement / Join reduction (`indep2_join_step`)**: If $G[U]^c$ is disconnected, partition $U = U_1 \cup U_2$ into a connected component $U_1$ of $G[U]^c$ and $U_2 = U \setminus U_1$. Then $U_1$ is complete to $U_2$ in $G$, so $\chi(G[U]) = k_1 + k_2 = k$ (`colorable_of_join_colorable`, `colorable_join_of_colorable`) and either $G[U_1]$ is $K_{k_1}$-free or $G[U_2]$ is $K_{k_2}$-free (`cliqueFree_join_or`). WLOG $G[U_1]$ is $K_{k_1}$-free (which forces $k_1 \ge 3$ by `ge_three_of_colorable_not_colorable_cliqueFree`). We then split $U$ using the induction hypothesis on $U_1$ (and `colorable_induce_split` on $U_2$ when $k_2 \ge a - 1$ and $k_2 \ge b - 1$).
    3. **Critical + Connected complement $\implies |U| \ge 2k - 1$ (`critical_and_connected_compl_card_bound`)**: Using Mathlib's Tutte theorem (`SimpleGraph.exists_isTutteViolator`) on the auxiliary graph `HStar G r` on $U \oplus \text{Fin } r$ (where $r = 2k - 2 - |U|$), any perfect matching of `HStar` would yield a $(k-1)$-coloring of $G[U]$ (`colorable_of_isPerfectMatching`). Thus `HStar` has a Tutte violator, which projects to a set $P \subseteq U$ satisfying the Berge–Tutte bound $2k \le |U| + o(G[U]^c - P) - |P|$ (`exists_p_odd_components_bound`). By `colorable_induce_compl_odd_bound`, $G[U \setminus P]$ cannot be $(k-1)$-colorable; by $k$-criticality of $U$, $P$ must be empty, and by connectedness of $G[U]^c$, $o(G[U]^c) \le 1$, forcing $2k \le |U| + 1$, i.e., $|U| \ge 2k - 1$.
    4. **Direct construction for $|U| \ge 2k - 1$ (`splittable_of_card_ge_two_k_sub_one`)**: If $|U| \ge 2k$, any subset $S$ of size $2a - 1$ leaves $|U \setminus S| \ge 2b - 1$, and both sides have chromatic number $\ge a$ and $\ge b$ by `colorable_of_indep2_card_lt`. If $|U| = 2k - 1$:
       - If $G[U]$ has an $a$-clique $S$, then $\chi(G[S]) \ge a$ and $|U \setminus S| = 2k - 1 - a \ge 2b - 1$, so $\chi(G[U \setminus S]) \ge b$.
       - If $G[U]$ is $K_a$-free, pick any $v \in U$. Since $\alpha(G) = 2$, the non-neighborhood $N_H(v)$ of $v$ is a clique in $G$, so $|N_H(v)| \le a - 1 \le 2a - 1$. Choosing $S \subseteq U \setminus \{v\}$ of size $2a - 1$ containing $N_H(v)$ ensures $\chi(G[S]) \ge a$ (by cardinality) and makes $v \in U \setminus S$ universal in $G[U \setminus S]$, forcing $\chi(G[U \setminus S]) = 1 + \chi(G[U \setminus (S \cup \{v\})]) \ge 1 + (b - 1) = b$.
